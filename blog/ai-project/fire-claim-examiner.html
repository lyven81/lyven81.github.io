Fire Claim Examiner

Tells claims examiners which clause governs and what to pay

Lee Yih VenAI Project

Fire Claim Examiner reads a lodged fire insurance claim against the policy wording and returns two things: the clause that governs it, and the amount to pay.

A claims examiner settles those two questions on every file. The policy runs 61 pages, the customer's schedule lists the endorsements they bought, and the answer depends on how the two fit together. Doing that by hand, with thirty files open, is where the working day goes.

The policy is indexed as 116 clause-level passages, each carrying its authority, its peril and its excess formula. Retrieval runs dense vector search over a Qdrant collection, a local cross-encoder rescores the candidates, and a precedence stage then orders them by authority: an endorsement on the customer's schedule takes priority over the base clause it varies, and one they did not buy does not.

The governing clause comes back quoted, alongside every other clause the determination touched. Six checks run in a fixed order and the tool reports the first one that stops the claim, so an examiner sees where a file failed rather than only what the verdict was.

The amount comes back line by line. On a bakery fire it allowed the oven, the proofer and the shelving, excluded the dough mixer where the electrical fault began, and applied underinsurance to one schedule item and not the other two. Claimed RM 168,200, payable RM 130,700, with the clause shown against every line and the arithmetic printed underneath.

The cases that make the build worth having are the ones that look payable. A burst pipe claim holds exactly the right water damage endorsement and still fails, because the shop lot had stood empty for 48 days and that endorsement excludes loss while the premises are untenanted. A flood claim with the flood endorsement in force fails because the written claim arrived on day 31, against a 15 day requirement.

Every determination cites the wording it rests on and every figure carries its working, so the file note can be written straight from the output.
