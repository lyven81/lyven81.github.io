Fire Claim Examiner

Tells claims examiners which clause governs and what to pay

Lee Yih VenAI Project

A tool for a claims examiner at a Malaysian general insurer. It reads a lodged fire claim against the policy wording and returns three things: whether the policy responds, which clause governs, and what to pay.

The usual build here is a chatbot over the policy PDF. That answers what the policy says and stops there. The questions that cost money are the ones after that.

An insurance policy contradicts itself on purpose. An endorsement exists to displace the clause it is written against, so an exclusion and the extension that overrides it are both maximally relevant to the same question. Similarity ranking returns them side by side with no notion that one governs the other. Retrieval runs dense vector search over a Qdrant collection with a local cross-encoder rescoring the candidates, and then a precedence stage reorders by legal authority. An endorsement that is not on the schedule is withdrawn from that tier, because it cannot displace anything it was never bought to displace.

Six claims test it and two are paid. One holds exactly the right endorsement and still fails: the shop lot had been empty for 48 days, and that endorsement excludes loss while the premises are untenanted. Another is a textbook flood with the flood endorsement in force, and it fails because the written claim arrived on day 31 against a 15 day requirement.

Order is the answer. Every claim runs the same six gates and the determination is always the first one that fails. Check the peril before the period and a well evidenced fire that expired eighteen months earlier gets approved.

The figure is computed in code with no model anywhere in its path. Excesses resolve to the lesser of two amounts, two can run in parallel in one claim, and underinsurance is applied per schedule item after exclusions rather than before. Reversing those last two changes one payable by RM 4,625.

It is scored against an answer key the deciding code never opens: six determinations correct out of six, and both payable figures exact to the ringgit. The two axes that are still unmeasured are named on the page rather than left out.
