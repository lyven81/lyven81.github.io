DF Auto Parts Distributors

A staff desk for an automotive spare parts distributor where a salesperson starts the work, the manager who owns the decision receives it with the data to decide, and code or a named person carries it out.

Lee Yih VenAI Project

A staff desk for DF Auto Parts Distributors, an automotive spare parts distributor in Selangor where seven staff in Sales, Finance and Warehouse serve 120 workshops and parts shops. A salesperson starts each order, the manager who owns the decision receives it with the data to decide, and code or a named person carries it out.

A discount, an order below the RM 300.00 minimum, an earlier delivery date or paying before dispatch is a manager's decision. Farid Iskandar, sales manager, owns discounts and small orders; Azlan Yusof, warehouse manager, delivery dates; Nadia Zulkifli, finance manager, the prepaid route. Each request has to reach the right one with the facts attached, and the order waits until every decision is on record.

The desk runs on Claude Haiku 5.5 through an MCP server, with four policies, the Product Catalogue and the Customer Directory as resources, and seven tools. The AI only chooses which tool to call. Around it sit three layers of code:

  • PreToolUse hook: checks the staff member, prices, the 15% discount maximum, stock age, credit and duplicates, then turns the first rule a manager decides into an escalation to that manager.
  • PostToolUse hook: applies policy to every result and flags an order total that does not match for reconciliation.
  • Back end: records each approval against its request and places an order once, only when every approval is recorded.

When Chloe Lim asks for 10% off 10 cartons of 5W-30 engine oil, nothing is placed, and the reply is one sentence written in code: "Sent to Farid Iskandar, sales manager." Farid's chat opens with the figures: RM 4,400.00 less RM 440.00, with RM 5,200.00 of credit available. Farid answers "Approved.", the approval is recorded for that order only, and Chloe is told the order can go ahead once the customer's terms are settled. When Daniel Wong tries to approve the same discount, the reply names who decides: "Only Farid Iskandar, sales manager, can approve a discount."

The pattern that keeps showing up: the decision stays with the person who answers for it, and the system's work is getting the right facts to that person and keeping the record.

The staff desk replays the whole working day step by step, with Approve, Decline and Confirm buttons on the same checks as the chat.
