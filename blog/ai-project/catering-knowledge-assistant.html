Catering Knowledge Assistant

Meal delivery assistant that shows every answer's source

Lee Yih VenAI Project

A knowledge assistant for a home kitchen that delivers 35 weekday meals a day in Shah Alam and Kota Kemuning. Every answer shows the line in the kitchen's own document that produced it.

The usual build here is a chatbot over a folder of PDFs. That covers the written rules and stops there. Half the questions this kitchen actually receives are not document questions at all.

Twenty-seven of the twenty-nine questions come from eleven documents, cut into 128 passages. Menus are chunked one passage per meal, so a dish is never separated from its date and price. Retrieval runs dense vector search over a Qdrant collection, a local cross-encoder rescores the candidates on a CPU at no API cost, and a relevance floor decides when nothing retrieved is good enough to answer from.

The remaining two have no document behind them. "How many meals do I have left" and "when does my cycle end" differ per subscriber, because a public holiday inside a cycle extends it by one service day. Twenty subscribers finish on fourteen different dates. Those route to six fixed parameterised queries over the subscription records, where the model chooses which of the six to ask and never writes SQL.

The gap between the two paths sits on the page. A document search takes 0.28 to 1.22 seconds. A record lookup takes about a millisecond. One is a similarity problem and the other is arithmetic, and treating both as retrieval is how an assistant ends up guessing at a customer's balance.

The page also separates what is measured from what is not. The retrieval, the timings and the reply checks were all produced by running the pipeline when the page was built. The wording of each reply is the kitchen's approved copy rather than something the model composed on the published run, which is stated on the page rather than left for a reader to assume.
