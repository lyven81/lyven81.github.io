Evaluate AI Retail Customer Agent

Measuring and improving how retail AI serves customers

Lee Yih VenAI Project

A retail assistant told a customer to report a damaged item within 48 hours, which is the shop's policy, and then asked for photographs and an order number, which are not.

Evaluate AI Retail Customer Agent measures how often that happens. An operations lead at an online retailer puts an assistant on the front line with the shop's delivery, return and refund rules loaded in, and checks it by reading a handful of chat transcripts each morning. The answers come back fluent and mostly right, so a small sample is hard to read.

The build runs twenty customer questions through the assistant and grades every answer against eleven written policies. Two graders do different jobs:

  • A model judge reads each response for accuracy, relevance, grounding and helpfulness, and names every claim the policies do not support instead of returning a score.
  • Ten of the questions turn on a value with one right answer, so RM5, five to seven working days and forty-eight hours are checked in Python with the same verdict on every run.
  • Claude and Gemini both judge, blind to which prompt produced the response, and their agreement is reported beside the result.

Before any of that counted, the graders themselves were marked. Fifteen answers were hand-written with their faults known in advance, ten carrying a planted fault of a stated kind and five clean controls. The judge found 10 of 10 planted faults and raised no false alarms on the controls, and returned the same verdict on every row of a repeat pass.

Then the same twenty questions ran against a second prompt, with the policy file hash-pinned and the questions, model, temperature, judges and rubric all held still. Responses carrying an unsupported claim fell from 11 to 5 on one judge and 10 to 5 on the other, and the two judges agreed on 35 of the 40 responses.

The interesting part is the row that did not move. The deterministic fact checks passed 9 of 10 in both versions. Measured on critical business facts alone, the rewrite reads as no change; the difference appears only once grounding is measured separately.
