Motor Claim Triage and QC Evaluator: Scoring Only the Fraud It Can Prove

A motor claim triage tool that grades only the part it can prove.

Most fraud scores are sold as a black box and never checked against the insurer's own claims. This one is built the other way round.

The Motor Claim Triage and QC Evaluator does two jobs and stays honest about which it can measure. It sorts every claim into one of five handling lanes: fast-track, standard review, complex or high-value, investigate (SIU), and repudiate-candidate. That part is operational routing, shown as a distribution. Only one axis carries a real label in the data, fraud, so only the fraud flags are scored.

gemini-2.5-flash reads the claim facts and returns a typed verdict (lane, fraud risk, confidence, reason) as strict JSON. It never sees the outcome. A short deterministic rule, not the model, makes the routing call: at or above the dial a claim goes to investigation, a repudiation candidate always reaches a person, and nothing is auto-denied.

Then the scorecard. Against the real FraudFound_P label it reports catch rate, flag accuracy, false-alarm rate, F1, and accuracy on the claims the model was most sure about, each with a 95% Wilson confidence interval. The data is the Kaggle vehicle-fraud set: 15,420 real claims, 150 sampled for the demo. Fraud runs near 6 in 100, too rare to measure in a small sample, so the demo rebalances to roughly 40 fraud in 150 and discloses it on the page.

The honest move is the restraint: five lanes look impressive, but four of them have no answer key, so the tool refuses to claim accuracy it cannot show. On a rare event a model can look 94% accurate by clearing every claim. The scorecard exists to catch exactly that.

Measuring an AI's output, not just producing it, is the part most demos skip.
