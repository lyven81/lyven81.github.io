What Influences Property Price

Where five highrise buildings sit in their price cycle

Lee Yih VenCase Study

A valuer pricing a unit in one of five Kuala Lumpur highrise buildings works from recent comparable sales, adjusting for how the market has moved and for how the unit differs. Read across all 249 sales at once, the five buildings price nothing alike.

Pooled together they look like one market, rising about 4% a year. Apart, they are five separate pricing problems, and the comfortable average hides most of them.

The going rates run six to one: Property 3 at about RM1,300 per square foot down to Property 4 at about RM210. A single market rate would misprice both ends.

Only one building is genuinely rising. Property 3 has gained about 8% a year in recent years, while Property 1, Property 4 and Property 5 have all lost ground and Property 2 has plateaued. The 4% market average is carried almost entirely by Property 3.

Age does not set the price. Property 3, completed in 1994, is the dearest of the five, while Property 1, finished in 2009 and the newest, sits near the bottom, and Property 4 at 1986 is the cheapest. Once size, unit type and the passage of time are set aside, what remains is the building's own standing: its location and the tier of buyer it draws.

Size is the one clean rule across all five: a unit 10% larger sells for about 11% more, with no premium in the per-square-foot rate for larger units.

Building, size and timing together account for about 89% of why prices differ, and letting each building keep its own trend is what produced that fit. The pattern that keeps showing up: a market average flatters most of its members. For a valuation, start from each building's own going rate, scale by floor area, and adjust for time in the direction that building is actually heading, up for one, down for three.
