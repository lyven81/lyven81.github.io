Building in the open.

Notes from recent case studies and AI projects. Each entry pairs with a full report or live demo. The 10 most recent of each type are listed here.

AI Projects

  • Delivery Delay Predictor (predictive operations). Scores every open order's delay risk before dispatch, and names the driver behind each at-risk order.
  • Courier CS Quality Console (CS quality measurement). Grades 1,200 courier support contacts, AI and human, and finds the high-stakes cases closed instead of escalated.
  • Seasonal Pricing Console (governed pricing) — In-browser console: the profit-max nightly rate per Airbnb listing by season.
  • Voucher Analyzer (promotion incrementality). Which vouchers truly drove sales, not just which sold, scored per category.
  • Property Lead Tracker (lead attribution) — Five lead channels, 1,140 enquiries: which one signs tenants, and at what cost.
  • Motor Claim Triage and QC Evaluator (fraud QC). Sorts every claim into four categories with a fixed rule, then scores the fraud flags on real outcomes.
  • Borrower Risk Evaluator (loan-default classifier) — Classifies real loans and scores its own accuracy live against the recorded outcomes.
  • Market Research Agent (governed location intelligence). Answers 10 shop-owner questions from a data pack and cached Maps, near-zero cost.
  • Lucy — Bloom House Concierge (governed florist agent) — Resolves routine asks, escalates sensitive cases to a human, declines the rest.
  • Klinik Dr Fang (governed clinic assistant) — Front-desk AI finds patterns over patient records but structurally can't leak data.

Case Studies

  • The Quiet Slide. A delivery-app shop's sales held steady while orders quietly fell 37%.
  • When Blooms Arrive Late. A florist's refund log blamed damage; 70% of the money was on orders that ran late.
  • One Brand, Four Reputations. One coffee shop reads as a leader on Google and a laggard on Facebook, 20 points apart.
  • Before They Leave. The riskiest 20% of bank customers held 65% of all the leavers.
  • What a Car's Worth. Four plain features explain 94% of a used car's price.
  • What a Ringgit Returns. Across 120 campaigns, spend level beat channel: every RM1 returned about RM3.21.
  • What Moves the Cooler. Four things you can see explain 93% of a store's daily drink sales.
  • Riding the Growth Curve. An online store tripled in three years; the risk is cash, not demand.
  • Plan for the Peaks. A 120-room resort looks steady yearly but is wrong every single month.
  • Know When to Reorder. A bookshop loses RM1.49M in sales to bad stock timing, not bad buying.
